The west-side cost of living: the master page

Every cost line this site tracks, gathered on one page — housing, taxes, utilities, internet, insurance — with links into the live data behind each number.

The lines, with the live data behind them

Housing: the corridor's observed rents live in the monthly Rent Report (with the Concession Index tracking real leverage) and the buy side in the Builder Incentive Index; the crossover math runs in the rent-vs-buy engine on our own data. Property tax: the full stack by place — 1.902% (Weatherford) to 2.353% (FW-in-Parker) — is on the tax table, with the exemption playbook covering the $140K homestead cut. Utilities: deregulated electricity (choose-your-own via powertochoose.org), water by city, and the real monthly numbers in the utility guide. Internet: fiber is address-by-address — the availability guide. Insurance: the honest line — Texas runs top-five nationally on home premiums (hail), covered for renters in the hail-insurance guide.

How the total compares — the frame that survives scrutiny

Against the coastal metros this corridor recruits from, the total stack is dominated by two lines moving opposite directions: zero state income tax (the per-state math is computed in the origin-market series — California, New York, Hawaii and five more, each with real brackets) against higher property tax and insurance on a far cheaper house. Against other Texas metros, the corridor's edge is the entry price itself: new construction in the $350–500K band inside a top-decile district. The everyday tier — groceries, gas, services — runs at ordinary North Texas suburb levels: unremarkable, which is the point. Where the corridor is genuinely not cheap: home insurance, summer electricity, and anything requiring a contractor in peak season.

Quick answers

Is west Fort Worth affordable?

By coastal-metro standards, decisively — no state income tax and $350–500K new construction in a top district. By Texas standards, it's mid-band: the property-tax stack (1.9–2.35%) and hail-priced insurance are the lines that surprise arrivals.

What are property taxes in west Fort Worth?

Total rates run from about 1.902% (Weatherford) to 2.353% (Fort Worth addresses in Parker County) before exemptions; the $140,000 school homestead exemption cuts the school line substantially. The tax table and playbook on this site carry the current numbers.
