Flying from the west side: the DFW and Love Field math

Published by West FW Living, an independent publication of Anastasia Weaver Media · Updated September 3, 2026

The honest drive-time table, the park-or-drop decision, and the routing tricks that make the corridor's airport story better than it looks on a map.

The drive-time truth table

From the Willow Park / Hudson Oaks interchange: Dallas Love Field typically runs 40–55 minutes (I-20 → I-30 or TX-183 depending on hour) and DFW Airport 45–65 minutes (I-20 → 820 → 183/360, or the northern loop). Aledo adds roughly five; Weatherford adds ten-plus. The spread is the story: off-peak, both airports are painless; a 5–6pm eastbound departure is the one window that punishes, and the fix is scheduling flights before 3pm or after 8pm departures from home. Rule of thumb that holds up: leave 2.5 hours before a DFW domestic departure, 2 hours for Love, and add thirty minutes for any 4–7pm start.

Park, drop, or ride

Driving and parking wins for trips of 1–4 days: DFW's remote/express lots and Love's garages price reasonably, and prepaid parking (both airports sell it online) removes the full-lot risk. The household drop is the corridor classic for longer trips — the round-trip costs an hour of a spouse's time against a week of parking. Rideshare from this far west prices like a small flight itself at surge hours; treat it as the backup, not the plan. Which airport: Love (Southwest's fortress) usually wins on total door-to-gate time for domestic; DFW wins on nonstop breadth — including the international board that makes the international relocation plan workable from out here. Pair with the commute tables for your specific address.
