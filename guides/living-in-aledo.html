Living in Aledo: the definitive guide

Aledo is the small town that became North Texas's most-demanded school district address — ~8,100 students in the ISD, a town that still reads like a town, and a housing market running from $360K starter-new to seven-figure acreage. This is the assembled file: every number we've verified, every profile we've written, in reading order.

1 · The money layer

The Aledo-area combined tax basis runs ~2.06% (city 0.3901 + Aledo ISD 1.2052 + county/other) — below Willow Park–Aledo's 2.09% and far below Walsh's PID-loaded 2.689%, which means Aledo proper is often the cheapest way to carry the district (full table). The $140K homestead exemption gives back ~$1,690/yr against the school rate; the county's Aledo-area median bill (~$8,300) reflects home sizes, not a mistake. Entry points, priced: Morningstar from $360,990 · Parks of Aledo phases · resale via Aledo Heights and the established streets · acreage via Bear Creek Ranch and The Grasslands (every subdivision profiled).

2 · The schools layer

The district IS the demand: 12 campuses including a structure movers misread — a standalone ninth-grade campus, two middle schools splitting town and growth sides (every campus explained). The rule that survives all growth: campus assignment is per-address and re-drawable; verify in writing before any contract. Renters get the same schools — the 3BR district board from ~$2,100 is the try-before-you-buy play.

3 · The growth layer

Read the trajectory in the filings: our pipeline tracker logs the corridor's registered projects, the district's Growth Committee just presented high-school capacity recommendations, and builder behavior ($25–40K packages) tells you supply is still being pushed. Growth means: incentives now, boundary redraws later, and a tax base that keeps funding what people moved for.
