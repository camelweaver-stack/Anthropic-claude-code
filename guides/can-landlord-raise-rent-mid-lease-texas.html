SHORT ANSWER: NOT DURING A FIXED TERM

Can a landlord raise rent mid-lease in Texas?

During a fixed-term lease: no — the rent your lease states is the rent, unless the lease itself contains a clause allowing changes (rare in standard residential leases, worth checking). Texas has no rent control, so the flip side is that at renewal, increases are unlimited. That makes the lease boundary the entire game.

What each phase allows

During the term: rent is locked by contract. Watch instead for the legal side doors — new or increased fees mid-lease also generally require lease authorization, so if a "technology package" or trash fee appears on month four's bill, ask where the lease permits it.

Month-to-month: rent can change with proper written notice — typically at least a month, per the lease or statute. Convenient flexibility, priced accordingly.

At renewal: anything is legal; everything is negotiable. This is where the corridor's math bites: concessions made year one cheap, and renewal reprices from the base rent. The counter-leverage is data — what the community currently advertises to new residents (our True Rent Table), what nearby communities offer (leaderboard), and the cost to the landlord of a vacant unit. The full script is in the renewal negotiation guide.

Educational overview of Texas law, not legal advice; statutes change and leases add terms. For a specific dispute, consult a Texas attorney or legal aid (texaslawhelp.org).
