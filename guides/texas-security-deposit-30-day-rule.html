TEXAS PROPERTY CODE, TRANSLATED

The Texas security deposit 30-day rule, explained

Texas law gives landlords 30 days after you move out to refund your security deposit or send an itemized list of deductions — and gives tenants real leverage when they don't. Here's how the rule actually works, and the two things renters do wrong that forfeit it.

How the rule works

Under the Texas Property Code, once you've surrendered the unit, the landlord has 30 days to return the deposit and/or provide a written, itemized accounting of any deductions. Deductions must be for damages beyond normal wear and tear or amounts the lease lawfully allows — not for ordinary carpet wear, faded paint, or routine cleaning to freshen a unit. A landlord who acts in bad faith can be liable for $100 plus three times the wrongfully withheld amount plus attorney's fees — which is why a clear written demand referencing the statute so often shakes a deposit loose without court.

The two renter mistakes that break the rule: first, the clock and the landlord's duty depend on you providing a forwarding address in writing — no address, no obligation to mail anything (you don't forfeit the deposit, but you stall your own claim). Second, skipping the move-in condition documentation: the deposit fight is won at move-in, with photos and the inventory form submitted inside your lease's window.

The practical playbook

Photograph at move-in and move-out; submit the condition form; give written notice per your lease; deliver your forwarding address in writing at move-out; then wait the 30 days. If day 31 arrives empty, send a written demand citing the 30-day requirement and requesting the itemization. Small-claims (justice court) handles deposit cases up to $20,000 and is designed for self-representation. And if you chose a deposit-alternative product instead of a deposit — none of this applies; those fees were never refundable, which is exactly the trade we flagged.

Educational overview of Texas law, not legal advice; statutes change and leases add terms. For a specific dispute, consult a Texas attorney or legal aid (texaslawhelp.org).
