THE MOST TEXAS INSURANCE QUESTION THERE IS

Does renters insurance cover hail damage?

Parker County sits squarely in hail country, so let's answer it cleanly: your belongings, generally yes; the building, never yours to worry about; your car, only through your auto policy. The details decide real money.

The three-way split

Your belongings — typically covered. Wind and hail are standard covered perils on most Texas renters policies. If hail breaks a window and soaks your electronics, or a hail-driven roof failure ruins furniture, personal property coverage responds (minus deductible). Verify wind/hail isn't excluded or carrying a separate, higher deductible on your specific policy — some Texas policies split it out.

The building — the landlord's policy's problem. Roof, windows, siding: not yours. Your job is reporting damage promptly in writing (maintenance request + email), which also timestamps things if interior damage to your property follows. If the unit becomes unlivable, that's your loss of use coverage paying for temporary housing.

Your car — auto policy only. Hail damage to vehicles requires comprehensive coverage on your auto policy; renters insurance never touches it. This is the quiet argument for the attached-garage rentals out here: in a serious hail year, covered parking pays for a chunk of its premium in avoided deductibles and claims.

Storm-season habits worth having

Keep the phone-video inventory current (five minutes, twice a year), know your wind/hail deductible before the season, photograph any damage immediately, and report building damage in writing the same day. Full policy basics live in the renters insurance guide.
