A DIFFERENT SYSTEM WITH DIFFERENT RULES

Income-restricted apartments in Weatherford & Parker County

Income-restricted housing is its own parallel market — different qualification, different waitlists, different search tools — and listing sites bury it. Here's how the system actually works locally and how to search it properly, whether or not it turns out to be your path.

How qualification works

Most affordable communities in Texas run on the LIHTC (housing tax credit) program: rents are capped for households under set percentages of Area Median Income — typically 50–60% AMI, with limits varying by household size. Weatherford falls under DFW-area AMI figures, so a household can earn a genuinely normal local wage and still qualify — many working families are surprised to learn they do. Separately, Housing Choice Vouchers (Section 8) travel with the tenant; Parker County-area administration handles waitlists that open periodically.

Where to actually search: the Texas Department of Housing and Community Affairs' vacancy clearinghouse and affordablehousing.com list LIHTC properties by county — Weatherford listings appear there that never surface on mainstream apartment sites (aggregators showed roughly a dozen income-restricted options in Weatherford as of this year). Expect income documentation and, at popular properties, a waitlist — apply to several simultaneously.

And check the unrestricted floor too

Run both systems in parallel: our tracked market's budget floor — College Park from ~$900, and deep-concession offers like Oxford's "2 months free" netting to the mid-$900s effective — overlaps LIHTC rent levels without qualification paperwork or waitlists. The income checker works the standard-market side; for the restricted side, the property's own AMI chart is the authority. If past credit or rental history is the obstacle rather than income, the second-chance guide is the better starting point.
