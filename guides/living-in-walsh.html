Living in Walsh: the definitive guide

Walsh is 7,200 acres of the most deliberate community-building in North Texas — Aledo ISD inside Fort Worth city limits, straddling two counties, with production homes mid-$500s to $900s, custom past $2M, its own elementary school, and the most misunderstood tax structure in the corridor. The complete decoded file:

1 · The tax structure (read this before the model home)

Total rate 2.689% — and the reason is the PID funding the infrastructure and amenity program: ~$0.36/$100 in newer planning areas after doubling from $0.18 in 2026, while planning areas 1–3 kept the lower ~$0.18. Same community, ~$1,000+/yr difference on a $600K home — so "which planning area?" is question one for any lot or resale. Dollar tables, the year-two reassessment, and the PID-prepayment negotiation play: the Walsh money file. Village-level buyer notes: the Walsh neighborhood profile.

2 · Schools inside the fence

Walsh Elementary sits in-community (walk-to-school priced like the amenity it is) feeding McAnally Middle → Daniel Ninth → Aledo High (campus guide) — verify per lot as boundaries evolve with buildout.

3 · The market layers

Production builders (advertising $25–40K flex cash this cycle — sharpest use: rate buydown or PID prepayment, never all design center) · resale inside earlier villages (where low-PID areas can beat new on total cost) · and the luxury/custom tier into seven figures (the luxury file). Renters note: Walsh itself is own-to-live; the district's rental entry runs through the tracked corridor communities.

4 · The honest trade

You pay the corridor's highest carry rate for its deepest amenity-and-planning package plus city-of-Fort-Worth services. For households that will use what the PID funds, it prices fairly; for those that won't, Aledo proper carries the same district at ~2.06% (that file). Run both in the calculator — both rates are built in.
