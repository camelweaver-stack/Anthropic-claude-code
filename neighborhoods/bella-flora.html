Bella Flora: acreage luxury on the Parker line

The corridor's acreage-luxury answer — gated estate lots with genuine land, inside Aledo ISD, where the tax math and the district combine into the pocket's real pitch.

What it is

Bella Flora is a gated estate community on the Tarrant–Parker county line southwest of the I-20/I-30 junction: one-acre-class lots (many larger), custom homes rather than production plans, and a rural-luxury character — long drives, mature oaks, horse-adjacent neighbors on the perimeter roads — that the newer gated products east of it don't attempt. Pricing has generally run from the high six figures for earlier builds to well into seven figures for newer customs on premium lots; as with every pocket at this tier, verify current comparables rather than trusting stale listings.

The file: the district-and-tax combination

The pitch that differentiates Bella Flora is the combination: Aledo ISD address + acreage + gate. On taxes, portions of the pocket fall outside any city's full levy, which can produce a materially lower total rate than Fort-Worth-in-Parker addresses — but boundaries here are parcel-specific, so the non-negotiable verification is pulling the actual taxing entities for the actual parcel (the property-tax playbook shows the CAD lookup). The $140,000 school-portion homestead exemption and 10% cap apply as everywhere. Utilities and internet are the second verification: acreage pockets mix well/septic/propane parcels with serviced ones, and fiber is address-by-address, not assumed. Schools: the Aledo ISD file covers the campus picture; this pocket's zoning has historically fed the district's western campuses — verify per address, per year.

How it compares

Against Montrachet: less polish, more land, and the district trade (Aledo vs FWISD-plus-private). Against Bear Creek Ranch and the acreage subdivisions: Bella Flora adds the gate and a higher custom tier. The full pocket map is in the west-side luxury file.
