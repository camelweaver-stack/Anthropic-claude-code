Master-planned communities: the collection

The corridor's plans-with-everything, compared honestly — amenity engines, HOA math, and which plan fits which family.

The plans, compared

Walsh is the flagship — the multi-decade Fort Worth plan with its own Aledo ISD elementary, market, gig fiber, and amenity campus; the fullest ecosystem and priced like it (the deep file). Morningstar runs the north-of-I-20 version in Aledo ISD — established phases, resale depth, and the amenity set without Walsh's price ceiling. Parks of Aledo is the trails-first plan (its greenbelt system is genuine infrastructure — see the file), and La Madera and the newer Aledo-side phases carry the current-builder wave tracked in the new-construction guide with the incentive index.

How to read a master plan before buying in

Four documents tell the truth: the HOA budget (amenity engines cost money — check dues trajectory and reserve funding, not just this year's number); the PID/MUD disclosure (several corridor plans carry assessment districts that sit on top of the property-tax line — the playbook shows where to find them); the phasing map (your quiet greenbelt lot may border phase 6's construction entrance for three years); and the school-zone letter for the exact address, because plan marketing and current zoning are different documents (the five-minute check).
