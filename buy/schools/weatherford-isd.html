Buying into Weatherford ISD: The value math nobody runs

School districts set the corridor's real estate gravity — and each one prices differently once taxes enter the math. The buyer's view of Weatherford ISD, numbers first.

The district math

Here's the corridor's quietest arbitrage: Weatherford ISD's school rate is 1.0342% against Aledo's 1.2052% — and Weatherford's total combined rate (~1.90%) undercuts Willow Park–Aledo's (~2.09%) by nearly two-tenths of a point. On a $400,000 home that's roughly $63/month, every month, forever — before the generally lower home prices even enter the math. For buyers whose campus research lands them comfortable with WISD's schools, the same monthly budget simply buys more house here.

Where that plays out: established neighborhoods near downtown and the college, the growing south-side subdivisions, and acreage on the edges — with Medical City Weatherford anchoring in-town employment (our shift-worker geography notes apply to buyers too). The district serves most of Weatherford proper while the city's eastern I-20 corridor splits toward Aledo ISD — the usual rule applies double where the boundary runs: verify the zoned campus for the exact address before contract. Rate context and the full city-by-city table: the tax page.

The three verifications (every district, every time)

1. Campus, not district: get the zoned campus for the exact address from the district in writing before your option period ends. 2. Rate trajectory: check the district's current rate and any pending bond or rate elections — growing districts change. 3. The exemption plan: file the $140K homestead exemption right after closing; at Weatherford ISD's 1.0342% school rate it's worth about $1,448/year.
