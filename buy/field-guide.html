The West Fort Worth Buyer's Field Guide

Published by West FW Living, an independent publication of Anastasia Weaver Media · Updated September 3, 2026

You asked for the playbook — here it is, one page, current as of July 31, 2026, refreshed monthly. Bookmark it; the link in your email always points at the latest edition.

1 · Know your real number before anyone else tells you one

Run the affordability calculator with the actual tax rate of the city you're targeting (they range 1.90%–2.69% here and change everything), then get a documents-reviewed preapproval. Check TSAHC/TDHCA programs before picking the lender.

2 · Pick the submarket by tax structure, not just school badge

Aledo ISD comes at three price-and-tax combinations: Walsh (~2.69% with PID, mid-$500s+), Aledo's communities (~2.06%, from $360,990), and the Willow Park pocket (~2.09%, mixed resale). The tax page and the community field notes carry the math.

3 · Time the incentive, not the market

Nobody times rates; you can absolutely time builder desperation. Watch the Builder Incentive Index monthly, target aged spec inventory, and remember the sharpest flex-cash uses: permanent buydown or (at Walsh) PID prepayment — never let it all vanish into the design center.

4 · Run the Texas process on its own rails

Option period is your everything-window — inspections (foundation always), negotiations, exit rights. The full sequence, including the wire-fraud minute at closing. Builder contracts aren't TREC forms; read accordingly.

5 · Check the rent math one last time

Before signing anything, run rent-vs-buy against our verified local rents. If renting wins today, this corridor's concession market is a genuinely good place to wait while the down payment grows — and the monthly Buyer Report will tell you when the balance tips.

This guide is educational publication content, not advice for a specific transaction. Verify every figure with the builder, lender, taxing authority, or program administrator — and enjoy the hunt.
