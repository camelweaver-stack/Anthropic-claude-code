Willow Park school zones, decoded for renters

Half the rental demand in this pocket exists because of one acronym: Aledo ISD. But Willow Park itself is split between districts, zones are drawn to the address — sometimes the street — and a leasing agent's "we're zoned to Aledo" is not a document. Here's how it actually works, and how to verify your unit in five minutes.

The Aledo ISD path from Willow Park

Aledo ISD's campus in Willow Park itself is McCall Elementary (400 Scenic Trail) — one of the district's K–5 campuses, and the school many Willow Park addresses feed. From elementary, the district's path runs through its intermediate and middle school campuses, the Daniel Ninth Grade Campus, and then Aledo High School. Which K–5 campus a given address feeds is set by the district's attendance-zone map, not by which school is nearest — so verify the campus, not just the district.

"Zoned to Aledo ISD" and "zoned to the campus you toured on Sunday" are two different promises. Only the district can make either one.

The five-minute verification (do this before signing)

1. Get the exact unit address — building and unit number, not the leasing-office address. In split-zone communities they can differ.

2. Check the district's attendance-zone lookup at aledoisd.org (and Weatherford ISD's if the table above says the community straddles), or simply call the district's administration office with the address — they answer this question all day.

3. Ask about capacity and transfers. Fast-growing districts occasionally overflow a campus and assign new enrollees elsewhere. Ask whether the zoned campus is accepting and whether any rezoning is proposed.

4. Get the enrollment documents list — a signed lease typically serves as proof of residency, which means enrollment usually can't be finalized until after you sign. Sequence your move accordingly.

5. Keep the answer in writing. An email from the district beats a leasing agent's verbal answer if anything is ever disputed.
