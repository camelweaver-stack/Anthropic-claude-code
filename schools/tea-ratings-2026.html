2026 TEA ratings for west Fort Worth schools

TEA published this year's A–F ratings two days ago. Here is what they say about the districts and campuses a west-side move actually puts you in — and the three limits that keep a letter grade from being a housing decision.

The short answer

The Texas Education Agency released its 2026 A–F accountability ratings on August 14, 2026, covering 1,202 districts and 9,105 campuses statewide (TEA release). For the west Fort Worth corridor, three things actually changed:

  • Aledo ISD held its A — 92 for the second year running, and the highest district score in the corridor.
  • Weatherford ISD moved up a letter, from a C (77) to a B (80) — the corridor's biggest district-level change.
  • Benbrook Middle/High School moved from a B to an A (90), inside a Fort Worth ISD that improved four points but stayed a C.

Who this actually applies to

Buyers shopping a corridor address where the school zone is part of the price — particularly in Willow Park, Hudson Oaks, Aledo and Walsh, where “Aledo ISD” carries a real premium. Renters comparing complexes along I-30 and I-20 whose leasing materials name a district. Families relocating into west Fort Worth who need a starting map before they can shortlist neighborhoods.

It applies less cleanly than people expect. A rating describes a school year that has already ended, at a campus your address may or may not feed. It is a useful input and a poor conclusion.

What a rating does not tell you

Three limits worth holding onto:

  • A district letter is not a campus letter. Aledo ISD is an A district containing two B elementaries; Fort Worth ISD is a C district containing an A middle/high school in Benbrook.
  • A campus letter is not your campus. Attendance zones are drawn to the address, sometimes to the street, and districts redraw them as the corridor grows. A leasing office or listing sheet naming a district is not a zoning document.
  • A rating is a rear-view number. It reflects the year that ended, and a single cycle's four-point move is noise as often as it is signal. Two or three years of direction is the more honest read, which is why the change column above exists.

Next steps

  1. Look up the exact address on TXschools.gov, which maps ratings to campuses and lets you search by address.
  2. Confirm the zoned campus in writing with the district itself — that is the only answer that binds.
  3. If you are renting, read Willow Park school zones, decoded for renters before you sign; the split-district problem is real in that pocket.
  4. If you are buying, pair this with the district hubs: Aledo ISD, Weatherford ISD, Brock ISD.
