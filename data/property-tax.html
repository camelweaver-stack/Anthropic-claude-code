Property tax rates: Willow Park, Weatherford, Aledo & west FW

Texas has no income tax; property tax is where it collects instead — and in this corridor the rate varies by nearly half a percentage point depending on which side of a city line and school district you land. On a $350K house that's real monthly money, and it's already baked into every rent you pay.

Buying? The rates are step one

The buyer's levers on top of these rates — the new $140K homestead exemption, the free annual protest, and the PID/MUD layer — are covered in the buyer's property-tax playbook.

How to actually read this table

The Aledo ISD premium is real and it's the schools. Willow Park–Aledo ISD at ~2.09% vs Weatherford at ~1.90% is roughly $56/month more on a $350K home — the direct price of the district that drives half this corridor's demand. Renters pay it too, invisibly, inside rent.

For buyers, the rate changes what a payment buys. The county's median tax bill runs about $3,309, but the Aledo area's median is far higher (~$8,300) because homes are bigger and rates higher — this is exactly why our rent-vs-buy calculator includes taxes rather than quoting a bare mortgage. Run it with the rate for the specific city-and-district combination you're considering.

The renter's angle: when your landlord's tax bill rises, renewal offers follow. Corridor rates and assessments have been climbing with values — one more reason the True Rent Table's base-rent tracking matters at renewal time.
