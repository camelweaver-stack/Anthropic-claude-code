THE NUMBER THAT DECIDES RENT-VS-BUY OUT HERE

Property tax rates: Willow Park, Weatherford, Aledo & west FW

Published by West FW Living, an independent publication of Anastasia Weaver Media · Updated September 23, 2026

Texas has no income tax; property tax is where it collects instead — and in this corridor the rate varies by nearly half a percentage point depending on which side of a city line and school district you land. On a $350K house that's real monthly money, and it's already baked into every rent you pay.

2026 adoption watch — what has actually changed so far

Taxing units adopt their new-year rates through late summer and fall. Confirmed as adopted, as of September 23, 2026: Aledo ISD, $1.1775 per $100 for 2026–27 (August 24 — a $0.0167 cut, the district's eighth straight; source: aledoisd.org); the City of Weatherford, $0.392246 (adopted September 8, unchanged from 2025 — the no-new-revenue rate again; source: weatherfordtx.gov); and Weatherford ISD, $0.9342 for 2026–27 (September 14 — M&O $0.7552 + I&S $0.1790, a full $0.10 below 2025's $1.0342, with the cut on the debt side; board action as reported by the Weatherford Democrat); the City of Willow Park, $0.429041 for FY 2026–27 — the no-new-revenue rate, down from 2025's $0.4325 (council adoption as reported by the Weatherford Democrat). And per the Tarrant County Tax Office's Truth-in-Taxation summary — the county's official adopted-rate record — White Settlement ISD adopted $1.1528 for tax year 2026 (M&O $0.6928 + I&S $0.46, down $0.0541 from $1.2069 — the corridor's largest school-rate cut this cycle) and Fort Worth ISD adopted $1.029099 (M&O $0.7869 + I&S $0.242199, essentially unchanged). That completes all four corridor school districts for 2026–27: every one adopted flat or down. With two of its three stack slices now adopted — city and Aledo ISD, both down — the corridor's flagship Willow Park–Aledo ISD stack is on track to shrink by roughly two cents once the county slice lands. Every 2026 adoption so far is flat or down — and the Weatherford side is falling fastest: once its county slice lands, the Weatherford stack drops roughly a dime while Aledo ISD's trims under two cents, widening the tax gap between the corridor's two school districts even as both cut. The dollar effect on a homesteaded bill is worked through in the homestead-exemption file. Close behind: Parker County has proposed its FY27 rate at the no-new-revenue level, $0.284719 combined, with the public hearing and expected adoption on September 22, 2026 — proposed, not adopted, until the commissioners' vote is confirmed (per the court's posted rate-setting actions). Still pending: the county-side slices — Tarrant County, the JPS hospital district, and Tarrant County College all show empty 2026 cells on the county summary, Parker County remains at its proposed $0.284719 — plus the cities of Aledo and Hudson Oaks. The full-stack tables roll to 2026–27 once the county slices land. The table above stays on the fully-published 2024–25 stacks until a majority of units adopt, so you are never mixing a new school rate with an old county rate.

Verify your own property in five minutes

Every number on this page is corridor-level; your bill is parcel-level. The authoritative path: look the address up at the Parker County Appraisal District or the Tarrant Appraisal District (the county line runs through this corridor — Willow Park, Hudson Oaks, Aledo and Weatherford are Parker; Benbrook, White Settlement and most of Fort Worth are Tarrant), read the parcel's list of taxing units and each unit's adopted rate, then subtract your exemptions. The Texas Comptroller's property-tax pages explain the exemption and protest machinery in plain language. Estimates on this page are educational, not tax advice or a bill.

Deciding between towns on the tax line? The head-to-heads put it in context: Willow Park vs Hudson Oaks (a ~0.8-point spread across one interchange) and Aledo vs Weatherford — and the neighborhoods hub maps which communities sit on which side of the lines.

Three different "tax rates" show up when you Google this — here's which one is yours

Searchers land on three numbers that look contradictory and aren't. (1) The city's own rate — e.g. the City of Weatherford publishes ~$0.40 per $100 and the City of Aledo adopted ~$0.39 for 2024–25 — is only one slice of your bill; it excludes the school district, county, and college levies that make up most of it. (2) "Effective median" percentages on tax-data aggregators divide median bills by median values, so exemptions and county-line quirks pull them below the sticker rate a new buyer will actually sign up for (the same town can show different medians on its Parker and Tarrant sides). (3) The combined adopted stack — the table above — is what a taxing-unit lookup for a specific address sums to before your exemptions. When you're comparing towns as a buyer, compare stacks; then subtract the $140K homestead exemption's district-by-district dollar value to estimate what you'd actually pay on a primary residence.

Buying? The rates are step one

The buyer's levers on top of these rates — the new $140K homestead exemption, the free annual protest, and the PID/MUD layer — are covered in the buyer's property-tax playbook.

How to actually read this table

The Aledo ISD premium is real and it's the schools. Willow Park–Aledo ISD at ~2.09% vs Weatherford at ~1.90% is roughly $56/month more on a $350K home — the direct price of the district that drives half this corridor's demand. Renters pay it too, invisibly, inside rent.

For buyers, the rate changes what a payment buys. The county's median tax bill runs about $3,309, but the Aledo area's median is far higher (~$8,300) because homes are bigger and rates higher — this is exactly why our rent-vs-buy calculator includes taxes rather than quoting a bare mortgage. Run it with the rate for the specific city-and-district combination you're considering.

The renter's angle: when your landlord's tax bill rises, renewal offers follow. Corridor rates and assessments have been climbing with values — one more reason the True Rent Table's base-rent tracking matters at renewal time.
